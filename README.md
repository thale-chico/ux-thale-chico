# Me, My Friend, and Pizza Pizza


## Intro
I have ordered food online several times either through an app or a website. While I prefer using a website for most of my food orders, I will download the app if it is somewhere I tend to eat more frequently and/or the deals are worth it. 

Currently, the [McDonald's](https://www.mcdonalds.com/us/en-us/full-menu.html) and [Taco Bell](https://www.tacobell.com/food) apps are installed on my phone for both of the reasons stated above. 

Recently, I installed the Little Caesars app because I saw an advertisement online for a $5 lunch deal. This piqued my interest because I like Little Caesars due to its price-to-performance ratio; it is *cheap* but *tasty*.

## Goal
A friend and I wanted to get a quick and easy lunch, so I suggested the lunch deal from Little Caesars. They agreed, so I used my previous experience of ordering food online to place our lunch order.  


## Interaction

### Initial Thoughts

This should be fairly easy, as most of the websites and apps I have used to order online have had a similar layout ***convention***, or commonly accepted or established practices, where similar items are grouped together. These items can be grouped in a few ways, but typically it revolves around a submenu. How fancy this submenu gets depends on the website or app. 

This demonstrates **"[Match[ing] Between the System and the Real World](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/#:~:text=2%3A-,Match%20Between%20the%20System%20and%20the%20Real%20World,-The%20design%20should)"**, one of the usability guidelines for using concepts in the real world. By using the familiarity of a regular restaurant menu, which typically has subcategories, it allows the interface of the app/website to also be familiar and learnable.  

Based on this usability guideline, I would imagine that the Little Caesars mobile app follows a similar ***convention***.

### Getting to the Menu

Getting to the menu required **4** easy steps:
1. select **ORDER** in the app
2. select **PICKUP** or **DELIVERY**
3. select your *location*
4. select your pickup *date* and *time*

I placed my order for pickup at *598 East 8th St. \#120* for as soon as possible (**ASAP** in the app), and then the menu was displayed to me. If you choose delivery, you are selecting the address for delivery.

### Searching the Menu

I was surprised when I saw the menu, as it did not follow the standard layout ***convention*** that I had come to know with other online ordering platforms.  

<center>

![image](https://hackmd.io/_uploads/H15NllbRzx.png)

</center>

From the image above, it is clear that items were still grouped together, as seen from the **LIMITED TIME OFFER**, **LARGE ROUND PIZZAS**, and **DETROIT-STYLE DEEP DISH PIZZAS** sections. Past these sections are the sides and drinks. The menu is a long list of items, and they were not hidden in submenus like the ordering platforms I have used before. 

My ***mental model***, or how I expected the app to function, did not match the ***conceptual model***, or how the app *actually* functioned. While it was a surprise, I think it is my favorite design of all of the apps and websites I have used. It leans on the usability guideline "**[Recognition Rather than Recall](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/#:~:text=6%3A-,Recognition%20Rather%20than%20Recall,-Minimize%20the%20user%27s)**" because you do not have to remember if that item or deal you wanted is under a certain submenu. I have certainly felt that frustration with the McDonald's app. By leaning on this usability guideline, it also reduces ***cognitive load***, or how much "brain power" is used as a user or consumer. 

### The Lunch Deal

While the Lunch Deal was not as cheap as I had hoped, I continued with the order, as it was still a fair price in my mind. Selecting the **Lunch Combo** option in the menu takes you to a selection page where you customize your lunch deal.

I chose Pepsi for my drink and a pepperoni deep dish for my pizza option, and added it to my order. My friend wanted the same, so I repeated the steps above.

### Checkout and Payment 

Sitting at the bottom of my screen was the checkout icon with the current price of my order. Selecting that icon led me to my order ticket where everything I ordered sat. Continuing to **CHECKOUT** led me to the checkout page, but not before a pop-up for Crazy Bread appeared. While I did not need to add it to my order, my friend and I decided it would be best if we did, in fact, add some Crazy Bread to our order. 

It asked if I wanted to check out with an account or as a guest. The account page is both a login and an account creation page. I logged in with Google, and continued to the checkout page. 

Looking at the checkout page, we see the relevant information before placing our order, such as another order summary, pick-up details (or delivery details if chosen), and payment method.

<center>

![image](https://hackmd.io/_uploads/r12lUeWAzx.png)

</center>

I added my credit card details to the app by clicking on the **Add a new card** icon, and this process followed the standard **convention** for adding a payment to an account. 

Finally, I clicked on the **PLACE ORDER** button at the bottom of the page. A splash screen appeared, confirming my order had been sent, and it said my order would take 15-20 minutes.  


## Outcome

Overall, interacting with the Little Caesars mobile app was very easy. Although it did not use submenus like other ordering platforms to organize its menu, navigating between the categories to find the items we wanted was relatively easy. 

The ***cognitive load*** was so low that my friend and I were able to focus on enjoying a cheap and delicious lunch.
